# What types of ISAs are there and what can I put in them?

The type of investments you can put into an ISA varies by both the type of ISA and what your account provider allows. 

> The lists in this article are not exhaustive and are not intended as financial advice.

## The most common types of ISAs

### Cash ISA

As you may expect from the name, you store cash in a Cash ISA. The provider pays you a percentage of your savings back into the account. In a normal savings account, you have to pay tax on these payments [above a certain amount](https://www.gov.uk/apply-tax-free-interest-on-savings/how-much-is-tax-free) depending on how much income tax you pay.

In a Cash ISA you can keep:

* Cash
* Some cash-equivalent assets

### Stocks and Shares ISA

Commonly shortened to "S&S", these ISAs can store what you usually keep in a General Investment Account as well as a few more niche investments. However when an investment is kept in a GIA, you pay [Capital Gains Tax](https://www.gov.uk/capital-gains-tax) when you sell it if there's an increase in its value (aka profit). That's not the case when it's kept in an ISA.

In a S&S ISA you can keep:

* Stocks
* Shares
* Fractional interests (a fraction of a share)
* Securities issued by companies and governments
* Mutual funds including certain overseas (OFR) and European (UCITS) funds
* Qualifying life insurance policies
* Depositary receipts (foreign stocks and shares)
* And some more niche eligible investment assets

### Junior Cash or S&S ISA

These accounts allow you to save and invest for your child, as an alternative to a Child Trust Fund. It converts to a normal ISA they control when they turn 18.

## Other types, including retired accounts

### Lifetime Cash or S&S ISA

Lifetime ISAs are supplemented with a 25% contribution bonus by the Government, but can only be used towards your first house purchase or to complement your retirement. There are limitations on all aspects of this account.

### Innovative Finance ISA

Certain peer-to-peer loans (direct borrowing of money to people via some apps), some types of crowdfunding and specific cryptocurrency-related securities can earn tax-free interest and returns. The investments held in these accounts are typically higher-risk and higher-reward.

### Help to Buy Cash ISA

Similar to Lifetime ISAs, these were cash savings accounts that would be topped up with a 25% Government-provided bonus to use for your first home. A limited amount of cash could be kept each month, and similar limitations to the Lifetime ISA applied.

These ISAs were taken off the market in 2019.

### Maxi and Mini ISAs

These were the first ISA products to hit the market back in 1999. They both could hold stocks, shares, cash and life insurance products. The difference between the two was the flexibility of who would hold your investments and who would hold your cash.

Maxi and Mini ISAs were taken off the market in 2008.
